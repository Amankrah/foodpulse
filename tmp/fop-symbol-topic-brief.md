## Topic + working title

- **Topic:** Canada's new "high in" front-of-package nutrition symbol — how to read it and what it means at the grocery store in 2026.
- **Working title:** "The new Canadian 'high in' symbol — what it means at the grocery store"

## Pillar

- **Slug:** `food-literacy`
- **Rationale:** The lens is decoding a packaging label. Per skill 1's pillar-taxonomy, Food Literacy covers "labels, claims, ingredients, additives, consumer education." This is a textbook Food Literacy piece.

## Brand mode

- **Mode:** Scientific
- **Rationale:** Regulatory citations (Canada Gazette, Health Canada, CFIA), numerical thresholds (15% DV), expected ≥4 citations. Default for Food Literacy with regulatory content.

## Reader profile

- **Primary reader:** Informed Canadian consumer, evidence-curious, time-constrained, values nuance. Likely doing a weekly grocery shop and has noticed the new black-and-white symbol on packaging.
- **What they bring:** Awareness the symbol exists. Some prior exposure to Nutrition Facts tables. May have seen confused or alarmist coverage.
- **What they want:** Plain-language explanation of what the symbol means, what triggers it, and how (or whether) to factor it into their grocery decisions.

## Angle / framing

- **Lens:** "What changed in 2026" + "Here's how to read it" — a practical literacy piece anchored to a regulatory shift.
- **Distinctive frame:** FoodPulse treats the symbol as information, not as a "don't eat" sign — fits the brand's #1 non-negotiable ("we don't tell you what to eat"). We explain the mechanism, surface the limits, and let the reader decide.

## Key questions the article will answer

1. What does the "high in" symbol actually mean — what triggers it?
2. Why these three nutrients (saturated fat, sugars, sodium) — and why this threshold (15% DV)?
3. How should you actually use it at the grocery store, including what it doesn't tell you?

## Contested areas to surface

- **Consumer impact of FOP labels — does it change behavior?** Evidence from Chile, UK Traffic Light, Mexico and EU Nutri-Score is mixed. Most rigorous evidence is on Chilean warning labels (positive effect on reformulation and some categories of purchase). For Canada specifically, evidence will be `preliminary` to `emerging` since the rule has been mandatory for only ~5 months.
- **Whether the 15% DV threshold is the right cutoff.** Some critics argue thresholds are too lenient; the regulation reflects Health Canada's evidence review. Not deeply contested in the policy-technical sense, but worth a transparent caveat.
